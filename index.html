The palladium catalytic cycle.

One cycle runs Suzuki, Negishi, Kumada, Stille and Sonogashira. Heck is different: no transmetalation, it inserts an alkene instead. Pick a reaction, then tap each step on the wheel.

Why palladium?Pd shuttles easily between Pd(0) and Pd(II), so oxidative addition and reductive elimination are both fast. Its organometallic intermediates are stable enough to form but reactive enough to turn over, it tolerates esters, ketones, nitriles and even water, works at mild temperatures, and its behaviour is tuned by phosphine ligands. Heck, Negishi and Suzuki shared the 2010 Nobel Prize for this chemistry.
